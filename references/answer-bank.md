# Answer bank

Build this once, keep it in the job-hunt folder as `answers.md`, and every
application form and every interview draws from it. It takes an afternoon and it
saves that afternoon back within two weeks.

The rule that governs all of it: **never name a thing you cannot defend three
levels down.** A term in a cover letter is a door, and someone will open it.

## Two registers

**Application forms take the written version.** Complete, specific, closed. A
form field is read once by someone deciding whether to keep reading, so a short
answer in a box that expects substance reads as indifference.

**Live and recorded interviews take the spoken version.** Sixty seconds that land
one claim and leave one obvious thread, then depth when they pull it. A written
answer delivered aloud is the most common way a strong candidate sounds
rehearsed.

Write both for every question in the bank. They are different answers, not the
same answer at two lengths.

## The numbers

Every number lives in the numbers table in `profile.md`, and nowhere else. Cite
it here; never restate it. Two rules follow from that. Each number is defensible
and sourced, because the interview will ask where it came from. And because
there is exactly one copy, the same claim cannot show up as two different
numbers across a CV, a letter and an interview, which is the thing that ends the
process.

A number an answer needs and the profile does not have goes into the profile
first.

Numbers arrive unprompted. No question is going to helpfully ask "by how much?"

## The behavioral bank

Write one story per line, in the person's own words, with a number in it.

- **Background, and why you fit this.** The trajectory as a deliberate line
  rather than a sequence of jobs.
- **A root cause you found that others missed.** Tests diagnosis, and whether
  they stop at the first plausible answer.
- **A prototype that changed someone's mind.** Tests whether they can move an
  organization, not just build.
- **A rollout where adoption was harder than the build.** The most revealing
  question in the set. Everyone has shipped something; not everyone has watched
  people refuse to use it.
- **Redesigning how something operates.** Tests scope of thinking above the task.
- **Working with no supervision.** Increasingly the whole job.
- **A project that did not work.** Requires a real failure with a real cost and a
  specific lesson. A disguised success ("I care too much about quality") reads as
  evasion and burns the credibility of everything said before it.
- **Why you are leaving.** Short, forward-facing, no grievance. Length here is
  read as unresolved feeling.
- **How you knew it worked.** Measurement. The question that separates people who
  ship from people who deliver.

Each story needs a number **and** an outcome. When a question lands unexpectedly,
the recovery move is to ask internally which story has both, and tell that one.
Never the most recent thing, and never one that did not work.

## Term to defence

For every specialist term in the CV, letter or profile, write a three-level
chain. This is the part of the bank that gets used most and built least.

- **L1**, the claim, one sentence.
- **L2**, "which one, specifically?" Names, versions, scope, what it excludes.
- **L3**, "how does it work / how is it measured?" The mechanism, the tradeoff,
  the thing that surprised them when they built it.

Worked shape:

> **L1** I run an evaluation over the routing classifier.
> **L2** Twenty-seven labeled task briefs with ground-truth tiers. It scores the
> routing decision itself, not the output of the model it routed to, because one
> evaluation over the whole pipeline cannot tell you which half regressed.
> **L3** Asymmetric cost model over-routing versus under-routing, reported as a
> lower bound, and it gates the weekly deploy.

Any term without an L3 comes out of the CV. This is the single most useful hour
in interview preparation, and the fastest way to find out which claims are
borrowed.

## Application form answers

Every free-text box on a form is this file's job. The field-shaped ones (dates,
years per skill, authorization, notice period, the demographic selections) live in
`forms.md`, built from `references/form-bank.md`, because a paragraph and a date
are not the same kind of answer and a file holding both becomes prose.

The recurring free-text fields, drafted once:

- Most relevant project, with what it does and what it changed.
- Depth in the two or three named technologies that keep recurring in the
  person's target postings. The number of years goes in `forms.md`; what the
  depth actually consists of goes here.
- Why this company. The one field that visibly cannot be reused, and the one most
  people reuse.
- Most impressive thing in your career.
- Salary expectation. See `compensation.md`.
  Research it; do not improvise into a box.

Where the person works in more than one language, keep the second-language
versions in the bank too. Translating live costs fluency that they actually have.

## Adapting by role type

Keep a short map: for each target role type in the profile, which story leads and
which claim gets held back.

The same career reads differently depending on which end is forward. The
long operational record leads for a role where operations is the rarest thing in
the candidate pool; the specialist work leads where the specialism is. Lead with
whichever is rarest **in the pool applying to that posting**, not with whichever
the person is proudest of.

When a claim is a personal project rather than client work, say so before being
asked. Volunteered context reads as confidence; the same fact extracted under
questioning reads as inflation.

## Delivery

- **Sixty seconds, one claim, one obvious thread.** Let them pull.
- **Stop when you land it.** Silence reads as confident. Continuing reads as
  searching for approval.
- **Do not restate the question while thinking.** Take the pause silently. It is
  shorter than it feels.
- **Do not narrate a correction.** Stop, restart the sentence clean, continue.
- **Numbers arrive unprompted.**
- **Do not volunteer a discount.** No apologizing for geography, gaps, age, or a
  non-linear path. State the fact once, in its strongest true form, and move on.
