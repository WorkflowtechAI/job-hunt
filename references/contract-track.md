# Track B: contract engagements

Work you propose to a company that never posted a job. It runs alongside the
application track off the same profile, and for most people it is the faster path
to money, because there is no queue and no comparison against two hundred other
résumés.

It is also the track people skip, because sending an application feels like
progress and sending an opener feels like exposure.

## Prospects, in nearness-to-revenue order

Work the list top down. The order is not preference, it is probability.

1. **Clients and employers who already know the work.** Anyone who has paid this
   person or managed them. The shortest distance to a signed engagement is a
   person who already trusts the delivery. Steer toward the work they have that
   matches what the person now does.
2. **Peers with overflow.** Companies doing the same work who are turning
   business away or missing a capability. The structure is subcontracting:
   mutual NDA, a free pre-scope of one real piece of work, then a per-item
   proposal. They keep the client relationship, the person does the build. Low
   trust required, fast to start, repeatable.
3. **Companies hiring for the work.** Every posting from Track A sourcing is also
   a prospect for the same work delivered as an engagement, especially the ones
   where the fit was good and the location or authorization was not. A company
   that cannot hire this person can usually contract them. Capture the company
   name during Track A even when the posted role scores poorly.
4. **Cold, but specific.** Companies with a visible problem the person can name
   from the outside. Lowest hit rate, and worth running only when the opener
   references something real and checkable rather than a generic capability.

## The opener

Short. Specific. Personal. Sent by a human.

Four sentences is a good target: something true and specific about them, the
problem it implies, one sentence of evidence that this person handles that
problem, and one low-cost ask.

- **The specific thing goes first.** Anything that could have been sent to a
  hundred companies gets read as having been.
- **One problem, not a capability list.** A menu asks the reader to do the
  matching. Nobody does the matching.
- **Evidence, not adjectives.** A named system, a number, a link.
- **A low-cost ask.** Twenty minutes, or a look at one thing, not a proposal
  review. The first yes should be cheap.
- **No attachments on a first touch**, and never a deck.

Write it in the person's own voice. Where a voice guide exists for them, use it.
An opener that sounds like software is worse than no opener, because it spends
the introduction.

## Follow-up

Two follow-ups, spaced, each adding something rather than asking again. A useful
link, a relevant thing that happened, a narrower version of the ask. Then stop
and move them to a long list.

Third-touch silence is an answer. Reading it as one keeps the pipeline honest and
the person's self-respect intact.

## Pricing

Quote a ladder, not a number: a higher rate for small committed blocks, a lower
rate for larger guaranteed volume. It gives the buyer a real choice, it prices
the person's flexibility honestly, and it keeps a negotiation from becoming a
single number defended down.

Set the ladder in the profile before the first opener goes out, and hold it.
Discovering the rate mid-conversation is how the rate ends up wrong. Where a rate
band stops holding across several conversations, change the band in the profile
deliberately rather than case by case.

Compensation research in `compensation.md` applies here too, including
quoting contract and employment as a calibrated pair.

## Cadence

Weekly target from the profile. A realistic shape is eight to ten personalized
openers across two or three focused blocks, which is two hours of real work, not
a background task.

Volume without personalization does not scale in this track; it just burns the
list. Ten researched openers beat a hundred merged ones, and the hundred cost
more.

## Guardrails

1. **Nothing sends without the person's explicit approval.** Every opener,
   follow-up and proposal is drafted for review and sent by them. No exceptions,
   no "this one was obvious." Test sends go to the person's own address.
2. **Honest claims only.** No invented client stories, no borrowed case studies,
   no implied engagements that did not happen. The first call finds out.
3. **No scraped or purchased contact lists.** Publicly available business contact
   details, used to send an individually written message, and a real
   unsubscribe path where the volume warrants one. Respect the local rules on
   unsolicited commercial contact.
4. **The exclusion list applies.** Companies the person ruled out stay out.

## Scoreboard

Openers sent, replies, calls booked, proposals sent, engagements signed. Review
on the same day as Track A.

By week three, a message that is not producing replies needs changing, or the
target list does. Sending more of it produces more silence, and silence is the
one signal that is easy to mistake for progress.
